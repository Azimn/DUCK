# DUCK v0.8 Agency Phase Notes

Status: supporting engineering notes, nonbinding.

Version 0.8 was intentionally treated as a broad development phase rather than a one-feature increment. The phase began with a minimal demonstration that an unresolved intention could later trigger an endogenous action without another user prompt, then expanded until the same mechanism had to survive competition, delayed eligibility, context dependence, threat, low energy, interruption, commitment resolution, process restart, satisfaction, abandonment, extended quiet time, and language lesion.

The development sequence exposed several useful design corrections. Early concern selection was too weak to reliably translate a selected prospective concern into its preferred action, so the agency contribution to action utility was strengthened while leaving safety and rest as legitimate competitors. Commitment follow-ups originally remained open when the underlying commitment resolved during a cooldown; commitment lifecycle closure was therefore moved ahead of cooldown gating. A long-horizon delayed-map scenario originally failed because the test intention had become too weak to clear the model's own salience threshold. The global threshold was not weakened merely to make the test pass. Instead, the scenario was corrected to represent a genuinely motivated delayed intention, preserving the distinction between remembering a weak whim and being motivated enough to act on it.

The phase also changed how boundedness is interpreted. A long sequence of quiet `wait` decisions is not automatically perseveration. Silence is a valid cognitive outcome. The bounded multi-concern tests therefore distinguish repeated active goal-directed behavior from quiet non-action and require diversity among active intentions without demanding arbitrary behavioral noise.

The current prospective concern representation is intentionally transitional. Private lifecycle metadata is encoded on self-reflective memory records because this lets the phase test persistence without adding a second canonical goal authority. A later implementation may replace that encoding if a clearer subject-owned prospective-memory representation becomes necessary. The behavioral distinction should remain: persistent concern, current salience, selected intention, action, outcome, and resolution are separate stages.

The strongest result of the phase is not that DUCK can schedule a future task. It is that unfinished intentions are now allowed to remain dormant parts of the subject and later regain causal influence according to current circumstances. A concern can lose to safety, wait for context, survive interruption, outlast a process restart, compete with another motive, or be relinquished when it becomes impossible, all while inner language is disabled.

The next development phase should preserve these gates and attack a new class of artificiality rather than simply adding more concern metadata. Candidate directions include endogenous formation of higher-level goals from lived experience, hierarchical planning and subgoal creation, counterfactual imagination, habit and skill formation, richer models of other people, or embodied opportunity recognition. Which of those becomes next should be determined by whole-organism simulation and human-perceptible failure rather than by feature count alone.
